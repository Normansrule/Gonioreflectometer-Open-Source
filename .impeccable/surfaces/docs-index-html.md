# Surface brief: OpenGonio website (docs/index.html, docs/lab.html)

Scope: the GitHub Pages site. index.html is Persuade (reviewers and material people decide it is real and worth a look); lab.html is Read/Operate (the existing simulator, BRDF explorer, calibration lab, scanner, equations), reskinned into the same world.
Audience: academic reviewers; graphics and material people. Action: open the repository / build guide; secondary: the lab and the paper.
Proof on hand: simulator outputs, CAD renders, the 3D simulator, the paper. No photographs of a built machine, no measurements: never implied.

## Direction contract

THESIS: A surface is read by moving one light across it, the way conservators use raking light. The page is that room: the visitor swings the lamp and watches mirror, black ABS and PTFE answer differently. Refuses the category default of a centred headline over a product render and three feature cards.
OWN-WORLD: Black-velvet ground (warm near-black), soft bone ink, one 3000 K tungsten light as the only accent (the light itself and the single primary action). Colour enters only through a conservation scale bar (white, 18 % grey, black, red, green, blue patches). Gloock display, Hanken Grotesk text. Square corners, no lines, no shadows at rest; tone steps separate surfaces.
STORY: See light behave on three real reference surfaces → understand that measuring this at every angle is the instrument's job → see how one measurement becomes a curve and a game material → see honestly what is proven → build it or open the lab.
FIRST VIEWPORT: Full-bleed live WebGL specimen (three vertical strips: mirror, black ABS with print layers, PTFE) lit by a lamp that follows the pointer with a spring and drifts slowly when idle. A 64 px rail on the exact vertical midline carries the h1 "OpenGonio, a printable gonioreflectometer", one sentence, two links and the one solid tungsten button "Get the build"; the rail docks as the header on scroll.
